# Transform Evidence Profile

Version: `hypertrophy_v1_9`
Implemented in code: `hypertrophy_v1_9` (`WorkoutGeneratorService.swift` -> `evidenceProfileCache.version`)
Last updated: `September 11, 2026`
Scope: `General hypertrophy-oriented bodybuilding programming for healthy adults using a fully equipped commercial gym`

This file is the programming contract for the workout generator. The goal is not to pretend the literature gives one perfect answer for every client. The goal is to make the app's assumptions explicit, versioned, reviewable, and adjustable.

## Source base
- ACSM 2026 Position Stand / umbrella review: Currier BS, D'Souza AC, Fiatarone Singh MAF, et al. "Resistance Training Prescription for Muscle Function, Hypertrophy, and Physical Performance in Healthy Adults: An Overview of Reviews." Med Sci Sports Exerc. 2026;58(4):851-872. DOI: `10.1249/MSS.0000000000003897`
- Broad hypertrophy literature on weekly volume, frequency, exercise selection, effort, rest intervals, and progression
- Lower-confidence deload/taper evidence and expert consensus for resistance training fatigue management

## Confidence scale
- `High`: broad directional agreement across strong reviews or large evidence summaries
- `Moderate`: strong practical support, but exact numeric prescriptions still depend on context
- `Low-Moderate`: useful coach-facing guidance with some evidence support, but less directly settled in the literature
- `Low`: mainly pragmatic coaching structure where the literature is sparse or indirect

| `BASE-001` | Baseline whole-body frequency | Programs should expose all major muscle groups to resistance training at least `2` days/week across the weekly plan | High | The ACSM 2026 position stand and public-facing summary both support training all major muscle groups on two or more days per week as a core healthy-adult prescription anchor |
| `FREQ-001` | Weekly frequency by priority | High priority muscles default to `2` targeted exposures/week; medium and low default to `1` targeted exposures/week, while the full plan should still satisfy `BASE-001` | Moderate | The ACSM review supports at least two weekly exposures at the whole-program level, but exact targeted frequency for specialization remains more context-sensitive and is still mediated by total weekly volume and recoverability |
| `SLOT-001` | Targeted exercise slots by priority | High priority areas default to `3` targeted exercise slots/week; medium `2`; low `1` | Moderate | This gives the generator a concrete way to distribute emphasis without forcing all stimulus into one session |
| `VAR-001` | Weekly exercise variation | Single-region priorities cap distinct primary exercises at `4` for high, `3` for medium, and `2` for low priority; broad composite areas apply that ceiling separately to real anatomical sub-regions, and repeated lifts should be preferred once a bucket is full | Low-Moderate | Stable exercises make progressive overload, skill retention, and performance comparison possible; the exact ceilings are pragmatic coaching guard rails, so catalog breadth belongs across mesocycles rather than being exhausted inside one week |
| `VOL-001` | Weekly direct sets by priority | High priority areas target `8-12` direct sets/week; medium `5-8`; low `3-5`, then adjust for volume/direct-work bias WITHIN the band — the adjustments (up to `+2.5`, down to `-1.5`) are clamped to the tier's bounds, so a band is a real limit | Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review strengthens the case that hypertrophy is enhanced by higher weekly volume, citing `>=10` sets/week as a useful anchor, but exact set targets still vary by muscle group, training age, and exercise choice. RAISING THESE IS PENDING: Baz-Valle 2022 (J Hum Kinet 81:199-210) recommends `12-20` weekly sets per muscle group for trained adults, and ACSM's `>=10` is the threshold at which hypertrophy is ENHANCED — a floor, not a target — so the High midpoint of `10` currently equals the MAINT-001 maintenance ceiling and a specialized muscle can receive the same volume as an ignored one. The raise to `12-16` is blocked behind the menu-sizing fix described in MAINT-001, because raising priority volume without it moves sets onto movements that are already too thinly dosed. The clamp is not cosmetic: unclamped, the bias adjustments return `12.5` for High, outside its own stated band, and any band whose top exceeds `maximumUsefulVariationCount * 4` demands more distinct exercises than VAR-001 permits |
| `FREQ-002` | Frequency must fit the chosen split | A priority's weekly targeted frequency may not exceed the number of training days in the CHOSEN split whose session style appears in that priority's preferred-style list. Where it does, frequency is cut to the achievable count; weekly direct sets are cut only where the surviving sessions cannot carry them, bounded by BOTH the per-session direct-set caps and the slot arithmetic of `SLOT-001` (about four working sets per movement). A split with no compatible day at all leaves the allocation untouched rather than clamping a priority to zero | Moderate | Frequency targets are set from the evidence bands before the split exists, and the split is chosen afterwards; nothing reconciled the two. A plan promised Lateral Deltoids three exposures on Push/Upper/Arms days inside an Upper/Legs/Push/Lower/Pull split containing two such days, and the repair pass then placed the third exposure on the PULL day — against the plan's own style list. An unreachable printed target also makes the Generator Lab misreport what was attempted |
| `MAINT-001` | Non-priority maintenance band | Muscle groups outside the blueprint's priority list are maintenance work targeting `6-10` direct sets/week, capped at `10` (`8` when recovery or nutrition adherence is constrained) and floored at `3` (`2` when constrained); zero weekly direct sets for a major muscle group violates `BASE-001` and must be surfaced. A group below the floor may be short of exercise SLOTS or of SETS, and the two need different repairs: if its movements already sit at their role defaults the group needs another weekly exposure, but if they sit UNDER their defaults one of the allocator's ceilings refused sets it was entitled to spend — the role default, the group's maintenance ceiling, a priority's weekly target, a per-session direct cap, or the day's fatigue cap — and adding an exercise makes that day worse. (Session-time budgets are not among them: nothing refuses training for projected minutes.) | Low-Moderate | The FLOOR is now `3` because that is the only maintenance dose anyone has measured: Bickel 2011 (Med Sci Sports Exerc 43(7):1177-87) held 16 weeks of hypertrophy for a further 32 weeks on a one-ninth dose — three exercises at one set, once a week, about three weekly direct sets — in young adults. `3` is also the largest floor this allocator can ever satisfy, because `canAddSet` caps a non-prime movement at its role default and that is `3` for an accessory, secondary or core movement in week 1. The previous floor of `4` was therefore unreachable by construction and fired on every generation. LOWERING THE CEILING IS PENDING, deliberately: Bickel supports roughly `4-6`, and a 2026-09-12 attempt to cut it to `6`/`5` was REVERTED after measurement — movements prescribed a single set went from 1 of 30 to 9 of 29 of the week, because the exercise MENU is sized before, and independently of, the set budget that must pay for it. The weekly volume was correct; the distribution was not. The larger ceiling was masking that flaw rather than avoiding it. The ceiling drops only once the number of movements a maintenance group holds is bound to the sets its budget can fund at a dose worth performing |
| `CONC-001` | Focus-day concentration allowance | Priority muscles may concentrate a larger share of weekly direct sets into a designated focus day than a simple even split would allow, but should still avoid putting essentially the entire weekly stimulus into one session | Low-Moderate | Real hypertrophy specialization often uses emphasis days rather than perfectly flat distribution; the validator should catch reckless piling-on, not reject every legitimate focus session |
| `VOL-002` | Weighted stimulus accounting | Direct work counts fully; indirect work is credited partially through weighted stimulus bonuses | Low-Moderate | This is a pragmatic approximation so the system can recognize that compounds contribute to multiple areas without pretending all stimulus is equal |
| `STR-001` | Strength-oriented loading anchor | Strength-focused anchor lifts should generally bias heavier loading, full ROM, multiple sets, and early-session placement | Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review found voluntary strength was enhanced by heavier loads (`>=80% 1RM`), complete range of motion, `2-3` sets, early-session placement, and `>=2` sessions/week |
| `ORD-001` | Exercise order | Anchor compounds stay first, secondary compounds follow, accessories after, core last. A day's focus area re-orders movements only WITHIN those bands, never across them — the "core-biased day" exception applies to a session containing no anchor compound, not to any session whose focus tag happens to be the core | Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review strengthens the case for early-session placement for strength outcomes, while progression tracking and fatigue management still support stable session order in practice |
| `SPEC-001` | Focus-day exercise specificity | A specialization day should be built around at least one prime hypertrophy movement for the target area; support, corrective, or scapular-control drills can complement the session but should not masquerade as the main growth slot | Low-Moderate | This is mostly biomechanics- and coaching-driven logic rather than a neat RCT rule, but it is important if the app is going to claim muscle-specific emphasis honestly |
| `CONT-001` | Week-to-week continuity | Comparable sessions should retain at least `1` anchor lift across weeks when the session style remains comparable | Moderate | Reliable progression needs repeatable anchors, but the program must still be free to rotate accessories |
| `REST-001` | Rest intervals by role | Anchor compounds: roughly `120-180s`; secondary compounds: about `90-120s`; most accessories: about `60-90s`; core/support drills: about `45-60s` | Low-Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review did not find rest interval duration to consistently impact primary outcomes in healthy adults, so the app should use role-based ranges instead of pretending one exact number is the evidence-based answer for every lift |
| `ECC-001` | Eccentric overload bias | Eccentric overload can be used selectively to increase hypertrophy stimulus for priority muscles when exercise tolerance and technique allow | Low-Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review reported that hypertrophy was enhanced by eccentric overload, but implementation details remain less standardized than broad volume/frequency guidance |
| `PROG-001` | Mesocycle progression | Week 1 establishes anchors, Week 2 adds volume/progression, Week 3 pushes hard sets while accessories bias toward `10-15` reps, Week 4 deloads while preserving movement continuity | Low-Moderate | This is a practical hypertrophy mesocycle template, not a universal law; the higher accessory rep bias in Week 3 shifts fatigue toward local/metabolic stress instead of piling more systemic loading onto the mesocycle peak |
| `DEL-001` | Deload strategy | Deload reduces fatigue primarily through lower hard-set exposure while keeping session identity and technical practice intact | Low-Moderate | Deload evidence in resistance training is weaker and less standardized than basic hypertrophy dose-response evidence; volume reduction is usually the least disruptive lever |
| `FAT-001` | Session fatigue accounting and caps | A movement's modeled session fatigue is LINEAR in its working sets: `sets x fatigueCost`. Session ceilings by style: Push/Pull/Upper `48`, Legs/Lower `48`, Arms `34`. Lower-body sessions tolerate more systemic fatigue than arms sessions | Low | The per-exercise `fatigueCost` values and the absolute caps are EXPERT JUDGEMENT, not evidence, and this row is rated Low for that reason: no published work assigns validated fatigue indices to individual exercises or establishes a session ceiling in any comparable unit. What the literature settles is the SHAPE and the ROUGH SIZE. Shape: nothing fits a curve to within-session fatigue against set count, and the diminishing-returns findings reached for here (Pelland 2026, Sports Med 56(2):481-505; Remmert 2025 SportRxiv preprint, NOT peer reviewed) describe diminishing BENEFIT — which belongs in a cap — not rising COST, which is what a convex exponent would assert. Size: Schoenfeld 2019 (Med Sci Sports Exerc 51(1):94-103) ran trained men at 5 sets x 7 exercises x 3 d/wk, 35 working sets per session, and that arm had the GREATEST hypertrophy, so a low session cap is unsupported; no trial has found a per-session set count at which hypertrophy gets worse. The caps above each permit about 24-26 working sets, roughly 10 per muscle, near Remmert's per-muscle figure of 11. Arms sits lower because an arms day funnels every set into two small muscles and passes that per-muscle figure fastest — NOT because single-joint work is cheap, which is false: Soares 2015 (JSCR 29(9):2594-9) measured 26.8% elbow-flexor torque loss after preacher curls against 15.1% after rows. The prior STEP model (full cost at set one, free at two and three, double at four, triple at five) contradicted PROG-001 outright: replaying the real constants, one unchanged full day cost `16 / 29 / 35 / 13` across weeks 1-4 against a Push cap of `19` and `18 / 30 / 34 / 12` against a Lower cap of `22`, so weeks 2 and 3 — the weeks the mesocycle exists to add volume in — were the only weeks that could not be built at their own role defaults. HARD LOWER BOUND on every cap: `seededDayFitsItsBudgets` projects a whole day at `minimumSetFloor` to decide whether it may take another movement, and in the linear unit a full day projects `29-30`, so a cap below that makes a full-size day unbuildable before one extra set is funded |
| `SAFE-001` | Report-bound joint-stress warnings | A joint accumulates modeled session stress only when the user's saved pain history, current pain check-in, or analysis reports a concern for that joint. A report that names a movement family charges only that family; a joint complaint that names no movement stays broadly cautious. The shoulder warning fires above `12`; elbow, lower-back, and knee retain their existing `14` thresholds pending real owner examples | Low | These scores and cutoffs are coaching guard rails, not clinical measurements. Report-bound charging prevents an unreported joint from changing the program, while the lower shoulder boundary makes the warning reachable by a planner-legal two-press session without rejecting two ordinary four-set presses |
| `REC-001` | Local recovery spacing | When possible, the weekly layout should avoid stacking two shoulder-intensive emphasis days back-to-back if a less-overlapping session can separate them | Low-Moderate | This is a recoverability and session-quality rule rather than a hard law, but it keeps the generator from creating obviously muddy upper-body sequencing |
| `SEQ-002` | Grip-variant within-day cap | Two exercises that are the same movement under a different grip or handle — identical movement pattern, identical primary muscles, and identical names once grip/handle wording is removed — count as ONE exercise, and a session carries at most one of them. Stance, unilateral and implement variants are explicitly NOT grip variants: standing and seated calf raises train different muscles, a single-limb version is a different demand, and changing the implement is ordinary useful variation | Low-Moderate | A live week spent six sets on `Lat Pulldown` and `Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown` back to back, at identical reps, rest, tempo and effort, in a week containing no rowing at all — the general two-per-pattern cap allowed it because they are exactly two. Grip width does shift regional emphasis somewhat, so this is a session-economy heuristic rather than a claim the variants are identical: with a limited number of slots, a second grip of the same movement buys less than a different movement would. A back-focused lifter can still train both grips in the same WEEK on different days |
| `SEQ-001` | Free-barbell squat within-day cap | A single session carries at most one free-standing barbell squat (Back/Front/Overhead/box/pause and similar axial-loaded barbell squats). Stable machine or guided squats (hack, pendulum, sissy, belt, Smith) and leg presses are exempt and can still accompany a barbell squat; other movement patterns keep the general two-per-session cap | Low-Moderate | Two heavy axial-loaded free squats in one session are largely redundant quad stimulus bought at high skill, spinal-loading, and systemic-fatigue cost; a second quad slot is better spent on a stable machine/press variant that adds volume without the same cost. This is a session-quality heuristic, not a hard physiological law |
| `SIMP-001` | Advanced-method restraint | The generator should not assume that failure training, complex periodization, special equipment, exotic set structures, or time-under-tension manipulation are necessary for strong outcomes | Moderate | The ACSM umbrella review found these variables did not consistently affect primary outcomes in healthy adults, so the app should treat them as optional refinements rather than core drivers |
| `TEMPO-001` | Explicit tempo prescription | Every generated exercise should carry an explicit four-part tempo, but the app should use broad role-aware defaults rather than overclaiming that one exact cadence is hypertrophy-optimal | Low | The ACSM umbrella review did not find time under tension to consistently impact outcomes. Tempo remains useful in Transform mainly as an execution-standardization and auditing tool, not as a high-confidence primary adaptation lever |
| `SLEEP-001` | Recovery tier from logged sleep | Recovery modulation is decided only from structured, dated sleep-log numbers (RecoveryState.swift): acute restriction (3-day average `<6h`, `>=2` days under `5h`, or post-call within `3` days) is Restricted; chronic mild shortfall (7-day average `<7h` across `>=3` logged days, or variability `>=1.5h`) is Constrained; adequate fresh data is Ready; missing or stale data applies NO adjustment and says so on the card and in programming notes | Low-Moderate | Chronic short sleep impairs recovery and, in a deficit, shifts loss toward lean mass; acute restriction degrades repeated-effort volume tolerance and motivation more than maximal strength. Exact hour thresholds are pragmatic anchors around the common `7h` adult floor, and the post-call trigger is a shift-work heuristic, not settled literature. Silent modulation from stale derived text is prohibited — that failure occurred and is the reason this rule requires dated inputs. Two deliberate boundaries: (1) subjective 1-5 sleep quality informs AI prompt context and trend display only, never the tier — duration evidence is materially stronger than subjective quality for volume decisions; (2) a wake-day counts as logged only when a main sleep or post-call recovery episode anchors it — naps add hours to an anchored day but a nap-only day is an unlogged night, excluded from all averages and day counts (and stated in the derived summary), so a logged nap can never read as a `1.5h` night and trigger a false Restricted tier |
| `SLEEP-002` | Recovery-tier volume action | Tier cuts happen in whole sets anchored to the `VOL-001` bands: Constrained caps each priority's weekly direct sets at its band midpoint; Restricted caps at the band floor with the cut directed at back-off/accessory hard-set exposure (preserve the first `1-2` hard sets per exercise and session identity per `DEL-001`), accessories biased about `1` rep further from failure, and loads NOT reduced; maintenance uses its `MAINT-001` constrained ceiling in both tiers | Low-Moderate | Flat percentage scalers frequently vanished in half-set rounding and cut first hard sets and marginal sets with equal probability; band-anchored whole-set caps are auditable and match fatigue-management practice (reduce hard-set exposure, protect intensity, since maximal strength expression is acutely preserved under short-term sleep restriction). Maintenance-on-low-volume evidence makes trimming the top of the range low-risk for retention |

## Operational notes

### Deterministic dosage ownership
- The pre-selected exercise menu owns both exercise identity and working-set dosage before any
  AI request. Loading weeks first attempt a whole-week reservation of existing role floors;
  priority-first funding remains the baseline. If a covered maintenance group misses its
  minimum, a second plan attempts minimum-first funding. It is accepted only if maintenance
  deficits improve without losing any non-priority direct work, priority direct/weighted work or meaningful frequency,
  crossing budget limits, or changing the menu. This does not change the MAINT-001 numbers.
  Unfunded minima are reported to diagnostic callers; comparing two greedy plans is not a
  simultaneous-feasibility solver for every objective.
  Every non-priority movement spends from the applicable major-muscle maintenance budget,
  including every group credited by shared-primary metadata. Unresolved admission searches
  retain the legacy allocator with an explicit console conflict; deload remains on that path.
- Recovery-tight maintenance groups are limited to at most four meaningful exercise identities
  under the 8-set ceiling. Fallback and AI output consume the same prescription; neither creates
  a separate role-default set budget or repairs maintenance volume after generation.
- The exercise catalog stays broad, but one weekly prescription stays narrow. Variation budgets
  count primary-target exercise identities, not every compound that gives a muscle secondary
  stimulus. Composite priorities such as Back use separate lat and upper/mid-back buckets.
- Priority feasibility counts prime hypertrophy slots, not token secondary credit. A prime slot
  may receive up to four working sets when weekly, per-session, fatigue, and time caps all allow
  it; this keeps `SLOT-001` arithmetic consistent with `VOL-001` without adding extra movements.

### Directional evidence vs exact numbers
The literature is stronger on directional principles than on one exact bodybuilding template. For example:
- higher weekly volume tends to support hypertrophy better than very low volume
- repeated weekly exposure is often useful
- exercise order matters for performance and progression
- heavy loading helps strength outcomes
- many advanced methods are optional rather than mandatory

The literature is weaker on exact app-ready numbers like:
- the perfect direct set target for every muscle in every client
- the exact percentage cut for all deload weeks
- the exact fatigue cap for each day style
- the exact tempo prescription that maximizes hypertrophy
- the exact rest interval that must be used for all exercises

That is why this profile marks some rules as `Moderate` or `Low-Moderate` instead of overstating certainty.

## ACSM 2026 Takeaways That Matter Here

The most useful upgrades from the 2026 ACSM position stand for Transform are:
- `BASE-001`: all major muscle groups should be trained at least 2 days/week at the program level, and a group's weekly volume must not come from a single movement pattern (a week that trains the back only from overhead, with no row, is a coverage gap even when the set count looks healthy)
- `VOL-001`: hypertrophy responds to higher weekly volume, with `>=10` sets/week being a practical anchor for muscle-growth planning
- `STR-001`: strength work benefits from heavier loading, full ROM, multiple sets, and early-session placement
- `SIMP-001`: the app should not oversell advanced methods, because training to failure, equipment type, set structure, time under tension, and periodization did not consistently change primary outcomes in the reviewed healthy-adult evidence

The most important caution from the paper is that few RT prescription variables changed primary adaptations consistently. That means Transform should lean harder on the big rocks:
- consistency
- sufficient weekly volume
- sensible exercise selection
- progressive overload
- recoverability

and be more humble about:
- exact tempo rules
- exact rest prescriptions
- claims that a specific mesocycle structure is "the evidence-based answer"

### Deload caution
Transform should not treat deload prescriptions as settled science. Deload logic should be conservative, explicit, and easy to revise. If future testing shows the app's deload weeks are too harsh or too mild, `DEL-001` should be updated and the code should be revised to match the new profile version.

### Change management
When a training rule changes:
1. update this file first
2. bump the version string
3. update the generator logic that references the affected rule IDs
4. note the behavioral impact in code review or release notes
